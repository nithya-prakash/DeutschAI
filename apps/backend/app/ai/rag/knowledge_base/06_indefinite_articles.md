# Indefinite articles (ein/eine)

The indefinite article ("a"/"an") also matches the noun's gender, but with only two
forms in the nominative:

- **ein** — masculine and neuter: ein Mann (a man), ein Buch (a book)
- **eine** — feminine: eine Frau (a woman)

There is no plural indefinite article in German — "some men" is simply `Männer` with
no article at all, or `einige Männer` if you want to say "some/a few" explicitly.

**Negating with kein/keine** follows the exact same pattern as ein/eine, and is the
standard way to say "not a" or "no" in front of a noun: "Ich habe einen Hund" (I have a
dog) becomes "Ich habe keinen Hund" (I don't have a dog / I have no dog) — not "Ich
habe nicht ein Hund." This is covered in more depth in the negation topic.

A common beginner mix-up: `ein`/`eine` change endings across the four grammatical
cases (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive), the same way `der`/`die`/`das` do.
In the nominative shown above, masculine and neuter share `ein`; in the accusative,
masculine becomes `einen` while neuter stays `ein` — this is the same der→den shift
covered in the Akkusativ topic, just on the indefinite article instead of the definite
one.
