# W-questions & yes/no questions

German has two question structures, distinguished by verb position.

**W-questions** (open questions) start with a question word, and the conjugated verb
comes immediately second:

- **wer** — who: Wer ist das? (Who is that?)
- **was** — what: Was machst du? (What are you doing?)
- **wo** — where: Wo wohnst du? (Where do you live?)
- **wann** — when: Wann kommst du? (When are you coming?)
- **warum** — why: Warum lernst du Deutsch? (Why are you learning German?)
- **wie** — how: Wie geht es dir? (How are you?)
- **wie viel(e)** — how much/many: Wie viele Geschwister hast du? (How many siblings
  do you have?)
- **welche(r/s)** — which: Welches Buch liest du? (Which book are you reading?)

The pattern is always: **question word → conjugated verb → subject → everything
else**. "Wo **wohnst** du?" not "Wo du wohnst?"

**Yes/no questions** have no question word — instead, the conjugated verb simply moves
to the very first position, ahead of the subject: "**Wohnst** du in Berlin?" (Do you
live in Berlin?) compare to the statement "Du wohnst in Berlin." (You live in Berlin.)
This verb-first inversion is the entire signal that marks it as a question — German has
no equivalent of English's "do you...?" auxiliary construction.

Answers to yes/no questions are simply **ja** (yes), **nein** (no), or — if answering
a negatively-phrased question affirmatively — **doch** (a dedicated word meaning "yes,
actually," used to contradict a negative question: "Kommst du nicht?" "Doch!" = "Aren't
you coming?" "Yes I am!").
