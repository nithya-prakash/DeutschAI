# Definite articles (der/die/das)

Every German noun has one of three grammatical genders — masculine, feminine, or
neuter — and the definite article ("the") changes to match:

- **der** — masculine: der Mann (the man), der Tisch (the table)
- **die** — feminine: die Frau (the woman), die Lampe (the lamp)
- **das** — neuter: das Kind (the child), das Buch (the book)
- **die** — also used for **all plurals**, regardless of the singular gender: die
  Männer, die Frauen, die Kinder

Grammatical gender is largely **not predictable from meaning** — `das Mädchen` (the
girl) is neuter, not feminine, because nouns ending in the diminutive suffix `-chen`
are always neuter regardless of what they refer to. This is why gender has to be
memorized per noun, ideally by learning each new noun together with its article
(`die Lampe`, not just `Lampe`) rather than the noun alone.

A few patterns do help predict gender:
- Nouns ending in `-e` are usually feminine (die Blume, die Schule)
- Nouns ending in `-chen` or `-lein` are always neuter (das Mädchen, das Fräulein)
- Nouns ending in `-ung`, `-heit`, `-keit`, `-schaft` are always feminine (die Zeitung,
  die Freiheit)
- Nouns ending in `-er` referring to people are usually masculine (der Lehrer)

The article also changes with grammatical case (see the Akkusativ note) — der/die/das
is specifically the **nominative** (subject) form.
