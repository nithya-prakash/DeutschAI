# Personal pronouns

German personal pronouns mark person, number, and (in the 3rd person singular)
grammatical gender:

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | ich (I) | wir (we) |
| 2nd informal | du (you) | ihr (you all) |
| 2nd formal | Sie (you, formal) | Sie (you all, formal) |
| 3rd | er/sie/es (he/she/it) | sie (they) |

**du vs. Sie** is the trap most beginners fall into. Use `du` with family, friends,
children, and other students. Use the capitalized `Sie` with strangers, service staff,
officials, and anyone significantly older in a professional context. `Sie` takes the
same verb form as the plural `sie` (they) — this is not a coincidence, it's historically
the same word used as a polite plural-of-respect. When in doubt, start with `Sie`; a
German speaker switching you to `du` is a signal, not an insult if you stay formal.

**er/sie/es** assign to grammatical gender, not biological sex: `der Tisch` (the table,
masculine) is replaced by `er`, `die Lampe` (the lamp, feminine) by `sie`, `das Buch`
(the book, neuter) by `es`. This is why German nouns must be learned together with
their article — you cannot predict a noun's gender from its meaning alone.
