# Prepositions

German prepositions each force a specific grammatical case onto their object, and this
is not predictable from the preposition's meaning — it has to be memorized per
preposition.

**Always accusative**: durch (through), für (for), gegen (against), ohne (without), um
(around/at [time]). "Das Geschenk ist für **den** Mann." (The gift is for the man.)

**Always dative**: aus (from/out of), bei (at/near — used for people's homes/workplaces),
mit (with), nach (after/to [places]), seit (since), von (from/of), zu (to). "Ich fahre
mit **dem** Bus." (I'm going by bus.) The dative case itself is introduced at the A2
level, but recognizing that these prepositions are "different" from the accusative ones
above is useful even at A1.

**Two-way (Wechselpräpositionen)**: an (at/on), auf (on/onto), hinter (behind), in
(in/into), neben (next to), über (over/about), unter (under), vor (in front of/before),
zwischen (between). These take **accusative** when describing motion *toward* a
destination (answering "wohin?" — where to?), and **dative** when describing a static
location (answering "wo?" — where?): "Ich gehe **in die** Küche" (accusative — I'm
going into the kitchen, motion toward) vs. "Ich bin **in der** Küche" (dative — I am in
the kitchen, static location).

**Time expressions worth memorizing early**: `um` + clock time ("um drei Uhr" — at
three o'clock), `am` + day/date ("am Montag" — on Monday), `im` + month/season ("im
Winter" — in winter). `am` and `im` are contractions of `an dem` and `in dem`.
