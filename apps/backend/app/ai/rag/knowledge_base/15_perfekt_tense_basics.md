# Perfekt tense basics

The Perfekt is the tense used in **spoken** German for almost all past-tense
storytelling — unlike English, where the simple past ("I ate") is the default in
speech, German speakers default to the Perfekt ("I have eaten") even for events long
finished. The simple past (Präteritum) exists but is mostly reserved for formal
writing and narration, plus `sein`, `haben`, and modal verbs, which commonly use their
simple past forms even in speech (war, hatte, konnte, etc.).

**Structure**: an auxiliary verb (`haben` or `sein`, conjugated to match the subject)
plus a **past participle**, sent to the end of the clause:

"Ich **habe** einen Film **gesehen**." (I watched/have watched a movie.)
"Ich **bin** nach Berlin **gefahren**." (I went/have gone to Berlin.)

**Choosing haben vs. sein**: most verbs take `haben`. Verbs take `sein` if they
describe motion from one place to another (gehen→gegangen, fahren→gefahren,
kommen→gekommen) or a change of state (aufwachen→aufgewacht, werden→geworden), plus the
verbs `sein` and `bleiben` themselves.

**Forming the past participle**:
- Regular ("weak") verbs: `ge-` + stem + `-t` — machen → **ge**mach**t**, spielen →
  **ge**spiel**t**
- Irregular ("strong") verbs: `ge-` + stem (often vowel-changed) + `-en` — this set
  must be memorized, e.g. sehen → **ge**seh**en**, trinken → **ge**trunk**en**, essen →
  **ge**gess**en**
- Verbs with an inseparable prefix (be-, ver-, ent-, er-, ge-, zer-) or ending in
  `-ieren` skip the `ge-` prefix entirely: besuchen → besucht, studieren → studiert
